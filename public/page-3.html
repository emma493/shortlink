Part 2 of 4 · A romantic story

One Evening in Lisbon

A short, sweet fantasy for anyone who believes the best things begin by accident.

5 min read Romance & Dating

The tram climbed the hill with a rattle that sounded almost like laughter. Mara held on to the brass rail, a paper bag of warm pastries in her other hand, and wondered for the third time why she had agreed to come to Lisbon alone.

Then the tram lurched, the bag slipped, and a stranger caught it before it hit the floor.

“I believe,” he said, holding the bag up like a trophy, “this belongs to someone with excellent taste.”

His eyes were the colour of tea with honey in it. He was smiling the way people smile when they have decided, in advance, to be kind.

They got off at the same stop. Neither of them mentioned it. They simply walked uphill together, past tiled walls the colour of the sea, while the evening turned from gold to rose.

The rooftop nobody told her about

“I know a place,” he said. It sounded like the start of a bad idea and the start of a wonderful one at the same time. Up four flights of crooked stairs, past a door painted the blue of a summer sky, a small rooftop opened above the city.

Strings of tiny lights swayed over mismatched chairs. Somewhere below, a guitar was being tuned. The whole river glowed like a ribbon of copper, and the air smelled of orange blossom and warm bread.

“Some people spend years looking for the right moment. Others just take the stairs.”

They shared the pastries. They traded the silly stories you only tell people you suspect you will keep. He admitted he was terrible at directions. She confessed she had once cried at a commercial about a lonely bear. Neither of them laughed at the other. They just laughed, together, in the same breath.

The song that finished the night

When the guitar below began a slow melody, he stood and offered his hand with exaggerated formality. “I should warn you,” he said, “my dancing is a crime against music.”

“Then we will be criminals,” said Mara, and took it.

They swayed between the chairs, barely moving, her head almost on his shoulder. The lights blurred. The city hummed. For three minutes nothing in the world needed to be fixed, explained or hurried.

When the song ended he did not let go right away, and she did not ask him to. “Tomorrow,” he said softly, “I know a place that sells the best custard tarts in Portugal.”

Mara smiled up at the glowing sky. Alone, she had told herself. Not anymore.
