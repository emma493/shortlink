The Letter at Midnight

A last little fantasy, and then your link is ready. Thank you for reading all the way here.

3 min read Romance & Dating

At midnight, when the house was finally quiet, Elena found the envelope on her pillow. Cream paper. Her name in handwriting she would have known anywhere, a little slanted, a little hurried, entirely his.

“I wanted to tell you something I am too shy to say out loud,” the letter began. “So I am saying it here, where I can be brave.”

She sat on the edge of the bed and read it slowly, the way you eat the last piece of something wonderful.

He wrote about the first time they met, and how he had walked home afterwards smiling at strangers. He wrote about the way she hums when she is concentrating, and how he pretends not to notice. He wrote that home was no longer a place for him. It was a person.

“I do not need a perfect life. I only need to keep choosing you, and I plan to, every single day.”

What a letter like that really says

It says that love does not have to be loud. It says that being seen, truly and gently, is the greatest luxury there is. And it says that somewhere out there, right now, someone is hoping to meet exactly the person you are.

Your own beginning

You do not need a rooftop in Lisbon or a letter on a pillow to begin. You only need a little courage, a little curiosity, and the willingness to say hello. The rest has a way of unfolding on its own.

Smile at someone. Reply to that message. Say yes to the invitation. Romance, like most wonderful things, starts with a very small step.
